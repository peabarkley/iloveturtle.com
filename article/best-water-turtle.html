Best Aquatic Turtles for Beginners: Five Species Compared

2026-11-19

Start with adult size, swimming ability and the space you can maintain for years. Juveniles sold in shops can be much smaller than adults.

Red-eared slider

Sliders are active, confident swimmers. Large adults need substantial water volume, strong filtration and a sturdy dry basking area. Check local rules before buying. See the red-eared slider guide.

Common musk turtle

Musk turtles stay comparatively small and often walk along the bottom. Give them underwater rests, easy access to the surface and a basking area. See the musk turtle guide.

Chinese pond turtle

Reeves’ turtles have three ridges along the shell when young. They need stable water quality, a dry basking platform and UVB. Adult males may darken with age. See the Chinese pond turtle guide.

Map turtle

Map turtles are active baskers with fine shell markings. Adult size varies by species and sex, and females can be considerably larger. Provide clean water and easy access to a dry basking area.

Yellow-bellied slider

Its needs resemble those of a red-eared slider. Plan for a large adult enclosure, strong filtration and reliable basking lights.

Which should you choose?

A musk turtle may fit a keeper with less space. A slider suits someone who can accommodate a large adult, while a map turtle or Reeves’ turtle may suit someone comfortable monitoring water quality. Set up and cycle the tank before bringing the turtle home.
