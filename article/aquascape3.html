Undergravel vs. Bog Filters: Which Suits a Turtle Tank

2026-11-19

An undergravel filter uses the floor of a turtle tank as its filter bed. A bog filter moves that bed into a separate planted area. Compare the two by deciding where solid waste will collect, how you will reach it and what happens if flow slows. Both can provide surfaces for biological filtration, but neither removes the need to test water or siphon debris.

How an undergravel plate handles waste

A perforated plate sits below the substrate. In a conventional setup, a lift tube pulls water down through the bed and returns it to the tank. The substrate provides surface area for bacteria that process ammonia and nitrite. It also traps food and feces, and the space under the plate is difficult to inspect. A digging turtle can uncover the plate or push the substrate into uneven piles, leaving some areas with little circulation.

If you already use a plate, keep the bed relatively shallow and accessible to a siphon. Use smooth particles that cannot be swallowed or pulled through the slots. Skip a plate if there is no safe substrate for your turtle or if you have to move heavy decorations to reach most of the bed. A reverse-flow setup can reduce the amount of solid waste pulled into the bed when the incoming water passes through a removable mechanical prefilter.

How a planted bog bed handles waste

In a common upflow design, a pump sends water below a bed of media. Water rises through the media and plant roots, then returns to the tank through an open outlet. Plants take up some dissolved nutrients as they grow, and the bed provides room for biological filtration. Set the bog where you can see the water level, reach the inlet and trim growth. A removable sponge or basket upstream keeps coarse waste from accumulating among the roots.

Plan for a blocked outlet and a power outage. Keep the normal water level below the rim, provide an overflow that drains safely back into the tank, and check how much water drains back when the pump stops. Verify that the turtle cannot reach the pump intake or pull loose plumbing. A simple drain or cleanout near the bottom can make it easier to remove sediment without emptying the entire bed.

Compare media by what it does and how it cleans

  • Coarse sponge: Use it before the bog bed or on the supply to a reverse-flow plate. It catches large particles in a location you can reach. Finer pads catch smaller particles but clog sooner, so use them only where they can be inspected and cleaned frequently.
  • Smooth gravel: In an undergravel bed, use a size that stays above the plate and is safe around the turtle. In a protected bog, rinsed gravel can support roots and keep the bed open. Avoid packing fine material into the flow path.
  • Porous ceramic media or rounded lava rock: These provide biological surface area downstream of mechanical filtration. Keep sharp pieces in a protected compartment, away from the turtle. Check that floating or shifting media cannot obstruct a return.

Choose a plate only if you have little external space and can clean the whole substrate bed. A bog is a better fit when you have room for a planted compartment and safe plumbing. If you are shopping for a standalone unit, see the turtle tank filter buying guide.

Set a maintenance schedule around flow and water tests

During the first few weeks, record normal return flow and check ammonia and nitrite regularly as the filter matures. Inspect the prefilter and outlet at least weekly initially, and check sooner after heavy feeding or plant shedding. Rinse a clogged sponge in water removed from the tank, siphon visible debris and adjust partial water changes to the test results. For an undergravel bed, clean sections in rotation so you can remove debris without disturbing the whole bed at once.

For a bog, inspect roots and the outlet as growth increases. Trim stems or roots that threaten to obstruct a pipe, remove dead leaves and clear the overflow. Once you know how quickly your system collects waste, adjust the service interval. Recheck the flow after changing the pump, plumbing or media rather than assuming the previous schedule still fits.

Troubleshoot restricted flow and clogging

If the return slows, disconnect power before opening equipment. Start with the accessible parts: the intake guard, prefilter and pump impeller. Inspect the tubes for a kink or debris and make sure the outlets are open. Restart after reassembly and confirm that the water level and return flow are back to normal.

On an undergravel plate, patchy flow often means waste has compacted in a section of the substrate or the lift tube is obstructed. Siphon the affected area in stages and inspect the lift tube. On a bog filter, a rising water level often points to a clogged outlet or root growth. Clear the return first, then check for compacted media. Test ammonia and nitrite after major cleaning and service biological media in stages when possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I choose an undergravel filter or a bog filter?

A plate can save space if the bed is safe for the turtle and easy to siphon. A bog filter needs room for a separate planted bed, an accessible prefilter and a safe overflow. It is often easier to service when the turtle produces a lot of solid waste.

Is a bog filter effective in a turtle tank?

A planted bog bed can add biological filtration and take up some nutrients. It works best with mechanical filtration ahead of the roots. Allow space for the bed and plumbing, and keep the outlet and overflow accessible.

Is undergravel filtration suitable for turtles?

It is a poor fit when the turtle digs heavily or when waste accumulates faster than you can siphon the bed. A shallow, accessible bed and a mechanical prefilter in a reverse-flow system can reduce the cleaning burden.

How do I calculate flow for a turtle tank filter?

Divide measured return flow by the actual volume of water. A return delivering 500 litres per hour into 100 litres turns over five tank volumes per hour. Pump output can fall with lift and resistance. Check actual return flow, water tests and whether the current is manageable for the turtle.

What plants can grow in a bog filter?

Use suitable emergent plants with roots that tolerate wet media. Verify the exact species, local restrictions and safety before buying. Keep the growing crowns above water and trim roots before they obstruct the return.
