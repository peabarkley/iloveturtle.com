How to Choose the Best Turtle Food: Brands, Pellets & Diet Guide

October 30, 2026 10 min read

Choosing a turtle food isn't about finding the most expensive brand. It's about matching three things: species, age, and pellet size. While commercial pellets are a great foundation, they should only make up 30–50% of your turtle's total diet. The rest should come from real foods. (For a full list of safe foods, see our guide on what turtles eat.) Let's break down the selection process.

1. Identify Your Turtle Type

This determines everything else. Buying the wrong type of food is worse than buying the wrong brand.

  • Aquatic Turtles (Red-eared sliders, Musk turtles, Snapping turtles): Omnivorous leaning carnivorous. They need higher protein and can eat floating or sinking pellets. See our Musk Turtle Care Guide.
  • Semi-Aquatic Turtles (Box turtles, Chinese pond turtles): Omnivorous. A mix of aquatic turtle pellets and vegetables works well.
  • Tortoises (Red-footed, Sulcata, Hermann's): Strictly herbivorous. They need high fiber and low protein, with grass/hay as the base. Read our Red-footed Tortoise Guide.

2. Read the 5 Numbers on the Label

Look at the Guaranteed Analysis on the back. Check these in order:

  • Crude Protein: Higher for aquatic turtles, lower for tortoises. This is the fundamental difference.
  • Crude Fiber: Must be significantly higher for tortoises. Grass sources should be near the top of the ingredient list.
  • Calcium & Phosphorus: Look at these together.
  • Ca:P Ratio: This is more important than absolute calcium levels. High calcium won't help if the ratio is imbalanced. (Learn about the consequences in our guide to Metabolic Bone Disease.)

⚠️ Always verify specific percentages against veterinary literature or the actual packaging. Also, remember that calcium absorption requires UVB light—see our UVB guide.

3. Choosing Aquatic Turtle Food

  • Floating vs. Sinking: Floating pellets make it easier to monitor food intake. Sinking pellets mimic natural foraging for bottom-dwellers like Musk turtles.
  • Pellet Size vs. Head Width: The pellet diameter should be smaller than the turtle's head width. Too big and they can't bite it; too small and it's a choking hazard.
  • Water Stability: Pellets that disintegrate instantly will foul the water, increasing filter load and risking eye infections or shell rot.

4. Choosing Tortoise Food

The core word here is fiber.

  • Grass ingredients (Timothy hay, alfalfa) should be listed first.
  • High-protein tortoise foods lead to rapid growth, pyramiding, and shell deformities.
  • Juveniles can tolerate slightly more protein, but adults must be kept on a low-protein diet.

5. Juvenile vs. Adult Food: Can You Use the Same?

Yes, but with adjustments.

  • Size: Use small pellets for juveniles, larger for adults.
  • Protein: Growing juveniles need slightly higher protein.
  • Transitioning: Never switch foods abruptly—it causes refusal to eat. Mix old and new food over 7-10 days. Always double-check water temperature during this time, as cold water causes appetite loss (see correct water temperature).

7. Five Common Misconceptions

  • Expensive = good: Price reflects origin and logistics, not suitability for your turtle.
  • Mixing brands is more balanced: Mixing long and short pellets encourages picky eating.
  • Only look at protein: High protein accelerates metabolic problems if calcium and UVB are inadequate.
  • Using fish/dog food: The nutritional profiles are entirely different and cannot be substituted.
  • Switching food instantly: Guaranteed refusal. Always transition over 7-10 days.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Can commercial pellets be the main diet?

They can be a staple, but should only make up 30-50% of the diet. The rest should come from real foods like leafy greens, aquatic plants, and protein sources.

Can baby turtles eat adult turtle food?

Yes, but you may need to soften the pellets if they are too large. Baby turtles also need to be fed more frequently than adults.

Should I soak turtle pellets in water?

Brief soaking is fine for babies or when switching foods, but long-term soaking will cause water-soluble vitamins to leach out.

Can tortoises eat aquatic turtle food?

No. Aquatic turtle food is too high in protein and too low in fiber for tortoises. Feeding it long-term can cause pyramiding and shell deformities.

How should I store turtle food?

Keep it sealed, away from light, and dry. Try to use it within a few months of opening to maintain nutritional value.

What if my turtle won't eat the pellets?

First, check the water temperature (cold water causes refusal to eat). Then consider if you just switched foods, or if there are other signs of illness.
