Ten Turtle Care Details Beginners Often Miss

2026-11-19

Good care begins with identifying the species and building a routine you can keep up over the animal’s lifetime.

1. Test water quality

Check ammonia and nitrite and track nitrate over time. Use a filter sized for the waste load, and make partial changes with conditioned water.

2. Keep temperatures stable

Measure water and basking temperatures separately. Select target ranges for the species and verify them with thermometers. See the water temperature guide.

3. Provide UVB

Use a timer for a regular day-night cycle. Follow the bulb manufacturer’s guidance on distance, barriers and replacement. See the UVB guide.

4. Match feeding to age and species

Use appropriate portions and a varied diet. Check body condition and remove uneaten food before it breaks down. See the feeding guide.

5. Provide a dry basking area and shelter

Use a stable platform the turtle can climb onto and dry off on. Offer hides with safe exits, including underwater resting areas where appropriate.

6. Plan carefully for winter

Brumation depends on species and health. Get species-specific guidance before attempting it and maintain normal conditions for animals that should not brumate.

7. Observe daily

Track appetite, swimming, breathing and basking. A sudden change or a soft, foul-smelling shell warrants advice from a reptile veterinarian.

8. Clean equipment and the enclosure

Remove waste and rinse biological media in removed tank water as needed. Stagger media cleaning so the entire biological filter is not disturbed at once.

9. Be cautious about tank mates

Turtles may compete for food and basking spots or injure tank mates. Have a separate enclosure ready and keep new arrivals apart while assessing their health.

10. Keep good records

Record weight, water tests, filter service and lamp changes. Know how to reach a reptile veterinarian before you need one.
