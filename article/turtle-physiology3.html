How Turtles See Color and Regulate Body Temperature

2026-11-19

Turtles can use vision to find food, detect movement and navigate. Their visual abilities vary by species, habitat and lighting.

How good is a turtle’s eyesight?

A basking turtle can notice movement above the water and retreat before a person approaches. Aquatic and land-dwelling turtles face different visual demands, so a single eyesight ranking tells you little about a particular species.

Can turtles see colors?

Turtle retinas contain multiple types of cones. Some species are sensitive to wavelengths that people cannot see. Color may help a turtle locate food or recognize other turtles. See the guide to turtle color vision.

Are turtles color-blind?

Turtles are not generally limited to black-and-white vision. However, eye anatomy alone does not show exactly how a given species experiences color, and lighting affects what it can distinguish.

Do aquatic turtles and tortoises see the same way?

Water and air affect how light reaches the eye. A turtle that searches for food underwater faces different conditions from a tortoise looking for plants on land. Keep the viewing environment in mind when comparing behavior.

Are turtles cold-blooded?

Turtles are ectotherms. They rely mainly on environmental heat and move between warmer and cooler places to regulate body temperature. When cool, they may move and digest food more slowly.

What this means for care

Give a turtle access to a suitable warm basking area and a cooler retreat, and measure temperatures in both places. Use species-appropriate lighting and UVB rather than assuming a bright-looking lamp provides the right conditions.
